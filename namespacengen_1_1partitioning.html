◆ cut_evenly_by_weight()

std::vector< std::size_t > ngen::partitioning::cut_evenly_by_weight ( const std::vector< double > & weights,
int num_parts )

Cut a sequence of weighted items into num_parts contiguous blocks of nearly equal weight.

The weight of the heaviest block is the least that any contiguous cut can achieve. Within that bound, each block in turn is cut as close as possible to an even share of the weight not yet assigned, preferring the heavier of two equally close cuts, so the blocks other than the heaviest are balanced as well. That balance is greedy: a block's share does not anticipate a heavier block later on.

Items of positive weight are what is cut. With at least as many of them as blocks, every block holds at least one; with fewer, each of the first blocks holds one, and the rest are empty. Each item of zero weight goes in the block of the item before it, or in the first block if no item comes before it.

A block's weight is taken as the difference of the prefix sums of the weights, computed in order, and the heaviest block is least among cuts weighed that way. Integer weights with a sum below 2^53 are summed exactly.

With equal weights, block sizes differ by at most one, as from cut_evenly(), though the larger blocks need not come first.

Parameters
weightsWeight of each item, each finite and nonnegative
Returns
num_parts + 1 offsets; block p holds items [offsets[p], offsets[p + 1])
Exceptions
std::invalid_argumentif num_parts is less than 1 or any weight is negative or not finite
std::overflow_errorif the weights sum to more than a double holds
